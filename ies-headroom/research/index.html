2. What Intuit has already shipped

I checked this first so the pitch builds on Intuit's work and does not repeat it.

  • July 2025: AI agents in QuickBooks and IES (Accounting, Finance, Payments and others), with human experts built into the experience. Source
  • July 2025: App Partner Program with tiered platform fees for developers. Source
  • February 2026: Intuit and Anthropic partnership. Businesses can build custom agents on IES with the Claude Agent SDK. Source
  • May 2026: Spring release with multi-entity close automation, intercompany eliminations, a dimensions API and HCM. Source
  • August 2026: Intuit Intelligence Chat for CFOs across entities. Source

What is still missing: nothing warns a customer that they are about to outgrow their setup, there is no verified store for third-party agents that can write to the ledger, and developers have no view of what IES customers need next. Headroom fills those three gaps.

4. Assumptions

Intuit does not publish these numbers, so I made estimates and list them here so they can be challenged.

  • IES has about 20,000 paying companies by 2027, with average revenue of about $25,000 per company per year.
  • About 30% of IES companies hit at least one major complexity trigger each year (new entity, new currency, new revenue model, audit).
  • Companies that hit a trigger churn at about 20% a year, roughly twice the mid-market benchmark.
  • Headroom can cut that to 12% within two years.
  • 30% of IES companies install at least one paid agent by year 3, with 1.5 agents each at about $9,000 a year.
  • Intuit experts can complete a standard review in about 15 minutes, which makes a $45 review fee work for the expert.
  • All company, person and agent names in the prototype are made up.

Key prompts

Research: "Why do companies switch from QuickBooks to NetSuite? Rank the reasons and give me the revenue band where it usually happens. Cite sources."
Avoiding repeats: "List every AI agent, marketplace and developer feature Intuit announced for IES from 2024 to 2026 with dates."
Critique: "Here is my idea list and RICE scores. Argue against my top pick as a skeptical Intuit VP would."
Prototype: "Build a clickable prototype of an IES screen that warns a CFO they are about to outgrow their setup. Use Intuit blue, show the evidence, and offer three fixes from lightest to heaviest."

What I rejected and why

  • An AI CFO chatbot as the main idea. Intuit shipped Intuit Intelligence Chat in August 2026.
  • Letting agents post entries on their own from day one. A CFO signs off on every number, so autonomy has to be earned one agent at a time.
  • AI-suggested app names and quotes. First drafts sounded generic. Rewritten to be specific to mid-market finance.
  • A 30% store commission. Too high to pull developers away from NetSuite. I used 15% on the first $1M.
